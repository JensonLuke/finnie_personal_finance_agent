## Framing it as a rate comparison

A useful way to think about this trade-off: paying off debt with a given
interest rate is mathematically similar to earning a *guaranteed*
return equal to that rate, since every dollar of debt avoided is a dollar
of interest you'll never have to pay. Compare that guaranteed "return" to
the expected (not guaranteed) return of investing that same dollar
instead.

## Why high-interest debt usually wins the comparison

Credit card debt commonly carries interest rates well above what a
diversified stock portfolio has historically returned on average over long
periods. Paying that off first is close to a risk-free "return" that beats
most realistic investment expectations -- which is why high-interest debt
paydown is so often prioritized ahead of investing beyond an employer
401(k) match.

## Why lower-interest debt is a closer call

A mortgage or student loan at a modest fixed rate is a different
calculation -- long-run average stock returns have historically exceeded
many such rates, though not with the guarantee that debt paydown offers.
Reasonable, financially literate people make different choices here based
on their own risk tolerance and preference for the certainty of debt payoff
versus the (higher expected, but uncertain) return of investing instead.

## Don't skip the employer match either way

The exception nearly everyone agrees on: if your employer offers a 401(k)
match, contributing enough to get the full match is usually still worth
doing even while carrying some debt, since it's an immediate, guaranteed
return that's hard for any other option -- debt payoff included -- to beat.

## Going deeper

This decision doesn't have to be all-or-nothing -- many people split
extra money between debt paydown and investing simultaneously, rather than
fully finishing one before starting the other, especially once the
highest-interest debt is handled.
