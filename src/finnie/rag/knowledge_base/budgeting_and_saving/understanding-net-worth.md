## A single number that summarizes your whole financial picture

Net worth is simply: everything you own (assets) minus everything you owe
(liabilities). It condenses a scattered financial picture -- a checking
account here, a 401(k) there, a car loan and a credit card balance
elsewhere -- into one number that reflects your overall financial position
at a point in time.

## What counts as an asset

Cash and savings accounts, investment and retirement account balances, the
market value of a home or car, and any other things of value you own.

## What counts as a liability

Any debt you owe: credit card balances, student loans, a mortgage balance,
car loans, and so on.

## Why tracking it over time matters more than the number itself

A single net worth snapshot is less useful than watching the *trend* over
months and years. A rising trend generally indicates you're saving more
than you're spending and/or your investments are growing; a declining trend
is worth investigating, whether it's from overspending, debt accumulation,
or investment losses.

## A word of caution about comparing to others

Net worth is highly dependent on age, income history, location, and life
circumstances -- comparing your number to a stranger's (or a
headline-grabbing average) is rarely a useful exercise. Tracking your own
trajectory over time is far more actionable than any external comparison.

## Going deeper

Net worth doesn't capture everything that matters financially -- cash flow,
job stability, and insurance coverage all affect your actual financial
resilience in ways a single net worth number doesn't fully reflect on its
own.
