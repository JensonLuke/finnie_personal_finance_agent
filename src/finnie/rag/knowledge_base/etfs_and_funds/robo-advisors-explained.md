## The basic idea

A robo-advisor is an automated investing service: you answer a
questionnaire about your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance, and an
algorithm builds and manages a diversified portfolio for you -- typically
using low-cost ETFs -- including automatic rebalancing over time, all for a
lower fee than a traditional human financial advisor typically charges.

## What they generally do well

- Building a diversified, low-cost portfolio matched to a stated risk level
  without requiring the user to research and pick individual funds.
- Automatic rebalancing -- periodically buying and selling to bring the
  portfolio back to its target mix as different holdings drift over time.
- Some offer automated tax-loss harvesting, a strategy that can be complex
  and time-consuming to do manually.

## What they generally don't do

Most robo-advisors don't offer the kind of holistic financial planning a
human advisor might -- estate planning, complex tax situations, or nuanced
life-event guidance. Some now offer hybrid tiers with human advisor access,
usually at a higher fee.

## How they make money

Typically an annual fee calculated as a percentage of assets managed,
separate from (and on top of) the expense ratios of the underlying ETFs
they invest in. Comparing the total combined cost -- advisory fee plus
underlying fund fees -- across services is worth doing before choosing one.

## Going deeper

A robo-advisor isn't a fundamentally different investment strategy than
what a well-informed beginner could build manually with a couple of
low-cost, broadly diversified ETFs -- its value is largely the automation,
discipline, and convenience, which can be worth paying for, but it's worth
knowing that you're paying for a service, not a secret strategy.
