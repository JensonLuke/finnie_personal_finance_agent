## Narrowing in on one slice of the economy

Instead of holding a broad slice of the whole market, a sector ETF focuses
on one specific industry -- technology, healthcare, energy, financials, and
so on. The idea is to let an investor make a targeted bet on, or gain
exposure to, one part of the economy specifically, rather than the market
as a whole.

## The trade-off: less diversification, more concentrated risk

A broad-market index fund spreads risk across many sectors, so weakness in
any single one is cushioned by strength elsewhere. A sector ETF gives up
that cushion on purpose -- if that sector underperforms, the fund
underperforms with it, with nothing else in the fund to offset it.

## When beginners are tempted to use them

Sector ETFs are often bought after a sector has already been in the news
for doing well -- a natural but risky instinct, since by the time a trend is
widely reported, much of the price move may have already happened. Chasing
recent sector performance is a well-documented pattern that tends to
disappoint more often than it rewards.

## A more measured use case

Some investors use a small sector "tilt" alongside a core broad-market
holding -- for example, holding mostly a total-market fund with a modest
technology-sector ETF layered on top, to intentionally overweight a sector
they have a specific view on, while keeping the bulk of the portfolio
diversified.

## Going deeper

If choosing a sector ETF, it's worth checking exactly which companies and
sub-industries it includes -- sector definitions vary between index
providers, and two "technology" ETFs from different providers can hold
meaningfully different companies.
