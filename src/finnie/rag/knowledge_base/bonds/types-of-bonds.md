Not all bonds carry the same risk or the same tax treatment. The issuer
matters a lot.

## Government bonds

Issued by national governments -- in the US, these are Treasury bonds,
notes, and bills, collectively often called "Treasuries." They're widely
considered among the safest investments available, since a government that
controls its own currency has a very high ability to make good on its debt.
Interest on US Treasuries is exempt from state and local taxes.

## Municipal bonds ("munis")

Issued by states, cities, or local agencies to fund public projects like
schools, roads, or utilities. Their standout feature: interest is often
exempt from federal income tax, and sometimes state tax too if you live in
the issuing state. That tax benefit is why munis can make sense for
investors in higher tax brackets, even at a lower stated interest rate than
a taxable bond.

## Corporate bonds

Issued by companies to raise money, often for expansion or operations.
Because a company is generally seen as more likely to run into financial
trouble than a national government, corporate bonds typically pay higher
interest than government bonds to compensate for that extra risk. Corporate
bonds are rated by agencies (like AAA down to junk/high-yield) as a
shorthand for how risky the issuer is judged to be -- a lower rating means
higher risk and, usually, a higher interest rate.

## Going deeper

A diversified bond fund typically blends government, municipal, and
corporate bonds so no single issuer's trouble can hurt you too much -- the
same diversification principle that applies to stocks.
