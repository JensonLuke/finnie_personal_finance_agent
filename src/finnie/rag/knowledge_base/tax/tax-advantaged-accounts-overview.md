"Tax-advantaged" just means an account type where the government gives you
a tax break to encourage saving for something specific -- usually retirement
or healthcare.

## The two flavors of tax break

- **Tax-deferred** (Traditional 401(k), Traditional IRA): you get a tax
  break now (contributions reduce taxable income), and pay tax later when
  you withdraw.
- **Tax-free growth** (Roth 401(k), Roth IRA, and HSAs used for medical
  expenses): you don't get a tax break on the way in, but qualified
  withdrawals are tax-free, including all the growth.

## Why the account type matters as much as the investment

Two people can hold the exact same index fund and end up with very
different after-tax outcomes purely because of which account it sits in.
This is why "which account" is often a more consequential first decision
than "which specific fund," especially early on.

## A quick mental checklist for beginners

1. Get any available employer 401(k) match first -- it's an immediate,
   guaranteed return that's hard to beat with any investment choice.
2. Consider maxing out an IRA (Traditional or Roth) next, since it usually
   offers a wider choice of low-cost investments than a workplace plan.
3. Only after tax-advantaged room is used up does investing in a regular
   taxable brokerage account typically become the next priority.

This is a general starting framework, not a rule that fits everyone --
income limits, employer plan quality, and personal goals can all change the
right order for a given person.

## Going deeper

A Health Savings Account (HSA), available if you have a qualifying
high-deductible health plan, is sometimes called "triple tax-advantaged":
contributions are pre-tax, growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for
qualified medical expenses are also tax-free.
