## Turning a loss into a small silver lining

Tax-loss harvesting means deliberately selling an investment that's down
in value to realize (lock in) a capital loss, which can then offset capital
gains elsewhere in your portfolio -- and, within limits, even offset a
portion of ordinary income -- reducing your overall tax bill for the year.

## How the offsetting works, roughly

Realized losses first offset realized gains of the same type (short-term
losses offset short-term gains, long-term offset long-term), then any
remaining net loss can offset the other type, and a limited amount of
ordinary income beyond that. Unused losses beyond the annual limit can
generally be carried forward to offset gains in future years.

## The wash-sale rule

This is the rule that trips people up most: if you sell an investment at a
loss and buy the same or a "substantially identical" investment within 30
days before or after the sale, the IRS disallows the loss for tax purposes.
This exists specifically to prevent people from selling purely to harvest a
tax loss while immediately buying back into the same position.

## A common workaround

Some investors sell a fund at a loss and buy a *similar but not identical*
fund (for example, swapping between two different S&P 500 index funds from
different providers) to stay invested in roughly the same market exposure
while avoiding a literal wash sale. Getting the "substantially identical"
line right requires care, and this is a good area to double-check current
guidance or consult a tax professional.

## Going deeper

Tax-loss harvesting is generally more relevant in taxable brokerage
accounts -- inside a 401(k) or IRA, gains and losses aren't tracked for tax
purposes in the same way, so there's nothing to harvest.
