## What owning a stock actually means

A stock (also called a share or equity) represents a small piece of
ownership in a company. When you buy one share of a company, you own a
fraction of that company -- proportional to how many total shares exist.
Owning stock doesn't mean you get a say in daily operations, but it does mean
you're entitled to a slice of the company's value and, in many cases, a share
of its profits paid out as dividends.

## Why companies sell stock

Companies sell shares to raise money -- for building factories, hiring
people, or paying down debt -- without having to take out a loan. In
exchange, they give up a piece of ownership and, often, a legal obligation to
report their finances publicly.

## Why prices move

A stock's price moves based on supply and demand: how many people want to
buy it right now versus how many want to sell. That balance shifts with
company news (earnings, new products), broader economic news (interest
rates, inflation), and simply investor sentiment. No single factor
determines price on its own.

## Going deeper

Stocks are generally categorized by market capitalization (large-, mid-, and
small-cap) and by sector (technology, healthcare, energy, and so on).
Diversifying across both dimensions is one of the most common risk-management
techniques beginner investors are taught -- see the
`risk_and_diversification` category for more.
