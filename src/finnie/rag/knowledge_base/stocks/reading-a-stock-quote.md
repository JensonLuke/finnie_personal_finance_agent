## What's actually in a quote

When you look up a ticker like AAPL, you'll usually see a handful of numbers
that can look intimidating at first but each answer one specific question:

- **Price**: what the stock last traded for.
- **Change / % change**: how much the price has moved since the previous
  day's close, in dollars and as a percentage. A green up arrow or red down
  arrow usually accompanies this.
- **Volume**: how many shares have traded hands so far today. Higher volume
  generally means more people are actively buying and selling.
- **Day range**: the lowest and highest price the stock has hit today.
- **52-week range**: the same, but over the past year -- useful context for
  whether today's price is near a recent high or low.

## "Stale" or "delayed" labels

Free quote sources are often delayed by 15-20 minutes, or the last quote
might simply be from whenever markets last closed (evenings, weekends).
A quote marked as delayed or cached isn't wrong -- it just isn't
necessarily the price you'd get trading right this second.

## Why the same stock can show different prices

Different providers sometimes show slightly different prices because of
rounding, delay, or which exchange's data they're pulling from. These
differences are normally tiny and not something to worry about for
long-term investing decisions.

## Going deeper

A quote is a snapshot, not a prediction. A stock's price reflects what
buyers and sellers agree it's worth *right now* -- it says nothing on its
own about whether that price is a good deal or where it's headed next.
