## What it is

Market capitalization ("market cap") is a company's total value as priced
by the stock market: share price multiplied by the total number of shares
outstanding. A company with 1 billion shares trading at $50 each has a $50
billion market cap.

## The common size categories

- **Large-cap**: roughly $10 billion and above. Typically established,
  well-known companies.
- **Mid-cap**: roughly $2-10 billion. Often past the earliest growth stage
  but with more room to grow than the largest companies.
- **Small-cap**: roughly $300 million-$2 billion. Can offer higher growth
  potential, generally paired with higher volatility and risk.

(These exact dollar boundaries aren't official and shift somewhat between
different data providers and over time as the overall market grows.)

## Market cap isn't the same as company size in every sense

A company's market cap reflects what investors are currently willing to pay
for it, which is influenced by growth expectations, not just current
revenue or number of employees. Two companies with similar annual revenue
can have very different market caps if investors expect very different
futures for each.

## Why this matters for diversification

Concentrating in only large-cap stocks (a common default, since they're the
most familiar) means missing out on the different risk/return
characteristics small- and mid-cap companies can offer. Many diversified
portfolios deliberately include exposure across the market-cap spectrum
rather than sticking to only the largest, most familiar names.

## Going deeper

Market cap is also how most major indexes are weighted -- in a
"market-cap-weighted" index like the S&P 500, larger companies make up a
proportionally bigger share of the index (and of any fund tracking it) than
smaller ones.
