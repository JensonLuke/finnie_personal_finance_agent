## Retirement accounts don't require a traditional employer

Freelancers, contractors, and small business owners without traditional
employees have their own set of retirement account options, generally
allowing much higher contribution limits than a standard IRA -- reflecting
the fact that self-employed people don't have access to an employer 401(k)
match or plan.

## SEP IRA (Simplified Employee Pension)

Relatively simple to set up and administer. Contribution limits are tied to
a percentage of net self-employment income (up to a cap set by the IRS,
adjusted periodically), making it a straightforward option for a sole
proprietor or small business with few or no employees. If you have
employees, you generally must contribute the same percentage for them as
for yourself.

## Solo 401(k) (also called an Individual 401(k))

Available to self-employed individuals with no employees other than a
spouse. It allows contributions in two capacities -- as "employee" and as
"employer" -- which can allow for higher total contributions than a SEP IRA
at the same income level in many cases. It also generally allows for Roth
contributions, which SEP IRAs traditionally have not.

## Which tends to suit whom

A SEP IRA is often simpler for a sole proprietor with fluctuating income
and no interest in a Roth option. A Solo 401(k) often allows higher
contributions at a given income level and offers Roth flexibility, at the
cost of somewhat more administrative setup (including, once the account
grows large enough, an annual filing requirement).

## Going deeper

These accounts' rules -- contribution formulas, deadlines, and filing
requirements in particular -- are detailed enough that self-employed
individuals often work with a tax professional or plan provider to set one
up correctly and confirm the current-year contribution limits, rather than
estimating them independently.
